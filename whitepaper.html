In plain English

Primordial is one shared dish of tiny creatures with real neural-network brains. Everyone who opens the site sees the same dish. You can release a creature with your name on it, and every hour it stays alive, its lineage earns a share of a prize pool paid in SOL.

The prize pool is filled by money from outside the game: part of the trading fees of the SOUP token and part of what people pay for disasters and food drops. The money you pay to release a creature is burned, not paid to other players.

The official mainnet Treasury, which receives the SOUP creator fees (70% goes on to the prize pool, 30% stays in the Treasury), is rr7VrmLv6akBeQTEDrMbL8n6G2zSdsvkvjT1iD8zKnJ . Anyone can check its balance and every transfer on Solscan.

This is a game with clear winners and losers. In our simulation of a medium-sized game, the average paid release got back 1.17 times what it cost, but the typical player got back less than they paid: about $3.16 for every $5. About 32 in 100 players came out ahead, and the top 1 in 20 got back 4.0 times or more. Players who release next to a warm vent and unlock traits do much better than players who release at random. Many players lose money.

Mechanics

  • The dish. Hundreds of creatures eat algae, split, mutate and form species. Each creature has 9 sensory inputs, 7 hidden neurons and 3 motor outputs. Nobody programmed their behaviour; selection is the only teacher.
  • Releasing. A release puts 40 siblings with a brain copied from the most successful living species, slightly mutated, into the dish under your chosen name. Paid releases cost today's release price in SOUP (or the same value in SOL, which is used to buy back and burn SOUP). New releases are protected from disasters for 10 minutes.
  • Recommended start. One button marks the warm vent with the most food and suggests the first trait, Efficient digestion.
  • Free microbe. For the first 500 Solana wallets. Connect and sign for a personal code, follow @primordialfun, name your microbe, then share your personal card on X by replying under or quoting our launch post with your code. We check X automatically every minute. Your X account must be at least 30 days old with at least 5 followers; one per wallet and per X account. Found posts reserve the microbe, and every reserved microbe can be released the moment $SOUP launches. A free lineage earns a quarter of a paid one.
  • Interventions. Anyone can buy a food bloom, meteor, cold front or toxic bloom. Each enters the public event log and plays out identically in every browser.
  • Evolution tree. When a released lineage reaches a milestone, its releaser can burn SOUP to unlock a trait such as armor, longer flagella, wider eyes or cold resistance. Some branches lock each other out.
  • Territory. The dish has named zones. The lineage with the most living biomass in a zone holds it. Holding zones adds a small bonus to hourly yield (see below).

Money flow

Payout formula

Every second, each released lineage is credited with its number of living creatures (creature-seconds). At the end of each hour:

hour budget B = pool balance x 0.5 / 24 yield Y = 0.85 x B bonus pot += 0.15 x B lineage weight w = (creature-seconds this hour) ^ 1.0 (0.6 before the v5 rule) x (1 + 0.05 x zones held, at most 3) x 0.25 if the release was free (all free releases together at most 10% of Y) lineage payout = Y x w / sum of all w (no wallet above 10% of a day's yield)

Once a day the bonus pot is paid 50, 30 and 20 percent to the three lineages with the most creature-seconds that day. A lineage must be at least 2 hours old to qualify, and territory does not count for the bonus. Then tomorrow's release price is set:

price = (yesterday's payouts to paid releases / yesterday's paid releases) / 1.0, kept between $1 and $25

The site shows it as "Today's release price $X, yesterday avg return Yx". Payouts under 0.001 SOL accumulate until they reach that size.

Fair limits

  • One free microbe per wallet and per X account, for the first 500 wallets, verified through a public post that contains a code tied to the wallet. The server batches all pending codes into one X search per minute and has a daily API spend cap.
  • At most 10 paid releases per wallet per day. No wallet earns more than 10 percent of a day's yield.
  • New releases ignore disasters for 10 minutes and burn energy at half rate while the lineage has fewer than 100 creatures, back to normal over 6 hours. Meteors have a 5 minute cooldown; cold fronts and toxic blooms 3 minutes.
  • The daily bonus uses the whole day's creature-seconds and a 2 hour minimum age, so a last-minute release cannot snipe it.
  • In simulation, even 1000 free lineages a day took about $32 a day under the 10 percent free cap and left honest returns unchanged; without the cap they took $268 a day. The X share requirement makes that many free claims impractical. A whale releasing 50 times more than everyone else got back about 0.4x.

Fairness and verifiability

  • The dish is a pure function of a public seed and the ordered log of paid actions. The simulation uses only exact arithmetic, so every browser computes the same world and checks its hash against the server every 500 ticks.
  • World hashes are committed on chain as memo transactions. Every payment, burn and payout is a public Solana transaction linked from the site.
  • Weather forecasts with a commit-reveal seed plus a future slot hash are designed but not live yet. Until they ship, disasters only happen when a player pays for one.

Survival update (v4 rule)

Released lineages used to die out within about 20 minutes, while the pool pays hourly. From a fixed tick recorded in the ledger, a newly released lineage starts at half the normal energy cost, rising evenly back to normal over 6 hours. Measured on a copy of the live dish with a release every 11 minutes: median lineage life about 1.2 to 1.5 hours, the unluckiest 1 in 10 gone within 8 to 35 minutes, the luckiest 1 in 10 past 1.7 to 3.4 hours. Rerunning the medium game with these lineages: average 1.21x, median 0.99x, luckiest 1 in 10 at 2.00x or above, about 50% of players ahead. The other half still lose. Sample: 14 measured lineages, so treat these as early numbers.

Balance update (v5 rule)

From a second fixed tick recorded in the ledger: the half-cost head start only applies while a released lineage has fewer than 100 creatures, so one release can no longer take over the dish (in tests a single early release had grown to about 97% of the dish in 5 minutes; with the cap it holds near 100). The hourly weight becomes creature-seconds to the power 1.0 instead of 0.6, and the daily release price targets an average return of 1.0x instead of 1.2x. Measured on 227 lineages from 16 runs on a copy of the live dish: median lineage life about 2 h 20 min, the unluckiest 1 in 10 gone within about 5 minutes, the luckiest 1 in 10 past about 3 h 15 min. Medium game: average 1.0x, median 0.88x, luckiest 1 in 10 at 1.26x or above, about 32% of players ahead. Most players lose a little; a third come out ahead.

Territory

The dish has nine zones. A rival needs 30 percent more living mass than the holder, and a zone changes hands at most once a minute. The measurements below were taken under the earlier rule, a 15 percent lead with no hold time. Measured with territory active, a released lineage held on average 0.28 zones over its life (the longest-lived held up to 1.9). A 5% bonus per zone therefore changes returns very little: the median moved from 0.71x to 0.70x. It rewards lineages that dominate the dish without letting them take over the pool.

Risks and disclaimers

  • This is not financial advice and not an investment. Payouts depend on other players and on outside money, and they are not guaranteed.
  • Most players should expect to lose some of what they spend. Simulated results are not a promise of future results.
  • Tokens can lose all their value. Smart contracts, servers and wallets can fail.
  • Paid games with prizes may be regulated or prohibited where you live. A free daily release is available, and the rules may change after legal review.
  • Parameters may be changed to keep the pool solvent and the game fair. Changes will be announced and shown on this page.
